Model assumptions
The human model now separates visual perception, choice/decision, auditory command recognition, language understanding, movement preparation and physical execution. Typical priors are centered near ~240 ms visual perception, ~170 ms auditory detection and ~150 ms movement preparation; choice time grows with the number of alternatives. These are sampled per person and per event rather than represented by one fixed reaction number. Defenders have individual walking speed, sprint speed and acceleration, so they do not jump instantly to maximum velocity. The speed control scales these research-based physical traits. Superman continuously estimates whether he can still beat the Mouse: he may begin by walking/jogging after it and then accelerate into a sprint, rather than launching at a fixed halfway percentage. Orchestrator includes the coordinator's visual scan/choice plus speech, the recipient hearing/understanding the call, and movement preparation. Loose strategies can still create duplicate movers, hesitation and retargeting. Mouse and defenders route around chairs and defenders may not touch or pass through the Mouse. The real-life start is preserved: Mouse enters from the left and the first vacancy is far to the right. Monte Carlo uses paired room layouts across strategies. Real observations are used as calibration references, not as forced target rankings.
