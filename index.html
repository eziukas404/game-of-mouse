Model assumptions
The physical world is fixed at exactly 20 m left-to-right and 10 m bottom-to-top, independent of screen size. Default setup is 17 chairs/people: one Mouse plus 16 seated defenders and one empty chair. The Mouse enters from the left and the initial vacancy is deliberately chosen far to the right. Human perception, auditory recognition, decision, movement preparation, walking, sprint speed and acceleration are sampled separately. Chairs and the Mouse are physical obstacles. Defenders may not touch or pass through the Mouse. Superman follows the real behavior you described: after the Mouse moves away he can stand, shadow/walk behind it, continuously judge remaining time and available speed, then accelerate and overtake on the clearer side. He is blocked only when both passing lanes are physically unavailable. Orchestrator includes visual choice, speech, hearing, understanding and movement preparation. Circular, Pairs, Zoning and Self-Organization can create duplicate movers, hesitation, retargeting and multiple vacancies. The stay-seated-near-Mouse rule can also be broken under chaos/panic. Real-life timings are calibration references, not forced target outcomes.
